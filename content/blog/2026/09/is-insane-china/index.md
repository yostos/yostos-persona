## What the "Enemy State Clauses" Are

They are provisions of the UN Charter, and they come down to the following three points.

- (Article 53(1)) When an ordinary grouping of states (a regional arrangement or an
  alliance) takes enforcement action against another state, Security Council authorization
  is required, but no authorization is needed against an "enemy state"
- (Article 77(1)(b)) Territory detached from an enemy state as a result of the war may be
  placed under trusteeship (a scheme in which some state governs it on behalf of, and under
  the supervision of, the United Nations)
- A reminder that what the Allies did to enemy states during and after the war, such as occupation and the disposition of territory, is not invalidated by the fact that the Charter came into being

## Where the "Enemy State Clauses" Stand Today

The purpose of the arrangement was to serve as a safety valve in the post-war settlement: if a former Axis state were to revive a policy of aggression in the years just after the war, it could be dealt with without waiting for Security Council procedure.

Over the following decades, however, it fell out of step with the international situation and has gradually become a dead letter.

1995
: At the 50th session of the General Assembly, marking the organization's 50th anniversary,
the clauses were recognized as "obsolete" in light of the changes in the world, and an
intention was expressed to begin an amendment to delete them

2005
: The World Summit Outcome document of 16 September 2005 expressed the resolve to delete
the "enemy state clauses"

Both were no more than expressions of intent to delete the clauses; the provisions themselves have never actually been removed. It is not that anyone opposes deletion. The permanent members voted for the 1995 resolution and agreed to the 2005 outcome document. What holds it up is the weight of the amendment procedure laid down in Article 108 of the Charter. A two-thirds vote in the General Assembly is required, along with ratification through domestic procedures by two-thirds of all member states, and those two-thirds must include all five permanent members. There is little motivation to go to that trouble for an amendment that merely tidies up wording, and once Charter amendment is on the table it cannot be discussed apart from Security Council reform, the hardest problem of them all. Since the clauses are effectively dead letters and the priority is low, no one moves on it alone.

## 「敵国条項」とは

国連憲章に書かれている内容で、主に次の3点を指します。

- (第53条1項) 通常国同士の集まり(地域機構や同盟)が他国に力づくの処置を取る際に
  安全保障理事会の許可が必要ですが、「敵国」については許可が不要である
- (第77条1項2号) 戦争の結果として敵国から切り離された土地は、信託統治（国連の監督
  のもとで、どこかの国が代わりに統治する仕組み）に置くことができる。
- 戦争中や戦後に連合国が敵国に対してやったこと、たとえば占領や領土の処分などを、憲章ができたからといって無効にはしない、という念押し
